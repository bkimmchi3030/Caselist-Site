# What Caselist can and can't access

**Last updated: November 9, 2026**

Caselist is a Chrome extension for debaters. It publishes your speech documents to openCaselist and shows you what the team you are about to debate has disclosed. This page is the short version of the [full privacy policy](PRIVACY.html).

There is no Caselist server. Everything it does happens in your browser, there is no account to create, and nothing about how you use it is counted or recorded.

## What it reads

From Gmail, if you connect it, with Google's read-only permission — it cannot send, delete or change mail:

- your Gmail address
- your recent round emails: chains with speech documents attached, Tabroom pairing emails and Tabroom file-share emails, including their subjects, dates, senders, recipients and attachment names, which is how it works out the tournament, the round, your side, your opponent and the judge
- the one attachment you choose, when you press Publish

It reads your mail when you open the side panel, when you search it, and when you open Settings — there, only the names and addresses on your round emails, to suggest your partner. While the panel is open it also checks about once a minute for new round emails, using the same searches; it does not read your other new mail. You can use Caselist without connecting Gmail at all, by uploading files instead.

From openCaselist, using the sign-in already in your browser, so it never sees your password: the caselists, schools, teams and rounds you can see there, including debaters' names as openCaselist lists them, and the Tabroom rounds linked to your openCaselist account.

## What leaves your browser

- To openCaselist, when you press Publish: the round you publish — tournament, round, side, opponent, judge, and any report or recording link you add — and its file. It goes on your team's openCaselist page, which other openCaselist users can see.
- To openCaselist, as you look teams up: the name of the team whose page is being read, the same as if you browsed the site yourself.
- To the developer, only if you type feedback and press Send: your message and the extension's version number, through a Google Form. Nothing else is attached, and Google's privacy policy applies to Google Forms.

No other part of your mail leaves your browser, and your mail is never sent to the developer.

## What it saves on your computer

In the browser's own extension storage, never on a server:

- your settings: caselist, school, team, partner addresses, how far back to look, tournament numbering, whether you use Caselist without Gmail, and which sections are open
- your Gmail address
- a record of up to 200 rounds you have published: Gmail's ID numbers for the email and the attachment, the team and the date, not any contents
- the ID numbers of up to 2,000 emails already checked and found not to be round emails, so they are not read again
- a list of openCaselist schools and teams, with debaters' names as openCaselist lists them

The contents of your emails are never saved. While the panel is open, mail it has just read is held in memory for up to five minutes and then discarded.

## How to delete it

- Removing the extension deletes everything it saved.
- To take back its Gmail access, go to myaccount.google.com/connections and remove Caselist Mailer.
- A round you have published lives on openCaselist; edit or delete it there.

## Check it yourself

- In Chrome, open chrome://extensions, find Caselist and click Details. It can reach three sites: gmail.googleapis.com, opencaselist.com and api.opencaselist.com.
- It asks for one Google permission, gmail.readonly, and no other.
- Signing in, Google will say the app isn't verified yet. Click Advanced, then Go to Caselist Mailer, then Continue.
